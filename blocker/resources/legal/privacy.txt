This Policy explains how calendar-blocker handles information when you visit
the website, connect a calendar, or submit scheduling requests.

1. Account information and Google authorization

When you connect Google Calendar, the Service identifies your account using
your primary calendar's email address. It stores that email, your provider
identifier, a refresh token needed for ongoing access, and the identifier of
any app-created calendar. Access tokens are used in memory. The Service does
not receive or store your Google password.

Google permissions requested are:
- calendar.readonly: read calendars and calendar events available to your account.
- calendar.app.created: create a separate calendar and manage events only on
  calendars created by the app.

The Service does not use these permissions to edit or delete events on your
existing calendars. You can decline authorization, although calendar scheduling
will then be unavailable.

2. Calendar information used for scheduling

For accounts named in approved scheduling requests, the Service reads calendar
lists and relevant events, including subscribed calendars unless excluded in
configuration. Unmanaged events are reduced to start/end times and busy status
for scheduling. Their titles, descriptions, locations, attendee lists, and
calendar names are not persisted by the app or included in GitHub proposals.
External-event snapshots remain in memory for the scheduling run and are not
kept as a historical calendar database.

The Service also processes its own managed-event identifiers, timing,
participant identifiers, and response status to reconcile bookings and handle
declines. These managed events and their details are stored in Google Calendar.
For connected people not named in a request, the scheduler may still read their
app-created calendar to clean up obsolete blocks. Account identification and
calendar-list checks can occur without reading their unrelated events.

3. Scheduling requests and GitHub publication

The Service processes the titles, descriptions, locations, attendee addresses,
durations, recurrence, time windows, priorities, and preferences you submit.
It uses them to propose configuration changes and create or update managed
calendar events. Shared-event details and attendee identities may be visible
to other participants and their calendar providers through invitations and
updates.

IMPORTANT: The default configuration repository is public:
https://github.com/dtrager02/calendar-blocker-config

Submitting a change publishes the proposed configuration to GitHub, including
its event details and attendee addresses. Your provider identifier and verified
account email are recorded in the pull request and commit message to identify
the requester. Other people, search engines, and repository copies may retain
that information. The app does not publish OAuth credentials or session
secrets to GitHub. The website displays approved requests that mention your
email, but the public repository itself is not restricted to those requests.

Maintainers review proposed changes. Removing a request from the active
configuration does not remove prior pull requests, commits, Git history,
repository clones, or past calendar events. Do not put confidential or
sensitive information in event settings.

4. Cookies, browser sessions, and temporary forms

The website uses essential cookies for sign-in security and authenticated
sessions. The OAuth state cookie lasts up to 15 minutes. The session cookie
lasts up to 30 days unless you sign out or invalidate it sooner. Server-side
session files contain a hashed session identifier, provider, email, expiration,
and a request-protection secret. Calendar refresh tokens are not placed in
browser cookies.

Form drafts, config snapshots, and submission receipts are held in server
memory. Forms expire after one hour; expired entries are discarded during
later cleanup or when the process restarts. The application does not include
advertising trackers or third-party analytics scripts.

5. Operations, infrastructure, and sharing

Connection and scheduling logs may contain account emails, request identifiers,
scheduling times, and operation/error information. The app does not intentionally
log OAuth tokens or unrelated calendar-event contents. Hosting and network
providers, including Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and Cloudflare, may process
IP addresses, request metadata, and security logs to deliver and protect the
website. Google and GitHub process information sent to their APIs under their
own terms and privacy policies.

The operator and authorized maintainers may access stored account records,
settings, proposals, and operational logs as needed for support, review,
security, and administration. Information may also be disclosed when legally
required or reasonably necessary to protect users, enforce the Terms, or
address fraud and security incidents.

The Service does not sell personal information, use it for targeted advertising,
or share it for cross-context behavioral advertising. Calendar information is
not used to train generalized artificial intelligence or machine-learning
models. Google's user data is used only to provide and support the requested
calendar and account functionality. The Service's use and transfer of
information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services
User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements:
https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy

6. Storage, security, and retention

The Service uses HTTPS for its public website and provider API connections.
OAuth credentials, account state, and browser-session files are stored on the
server with owner-only file permissions. These measures reduce risk but cannot
guarantee absolute security.

Refresh tokens and account/calendar identifiers are retained until removed by
the operator; revoking access does not automatically erase those files.
Session expiration prevents further authentication, but an expired session file
may remain until checked again or removed by the operator. Operational logs
and local config checkouts do not currently have an app-enforced automatic
deletion schedule. Contact the operator to request deletion of records under
the operator's control. Records may be retained where needed for applicable
legal requirements, security, or dispute resolution.

GitHub history and Google Calendar events have separate retention controlled
by those services, repository administrators, account holders, and any copies
made by others. Complete removal of publicly distributed information cannot
be guaranteed.
