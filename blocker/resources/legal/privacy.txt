1. Information collected and its sources

Account and authorization information. Google Calendar supplies your primary
calendar's email address when you connect. We use it with a provider identifier
to identify your account and the event settings that mention you. We store a
Google refresh token so scheduled runs can obtain access without asking you to
sign in each time. Access tokens are used in process memory. Google handles
your Google password; the app does not receive it.

Calendar information. Google supplies the list of calendars available to your
account, their identifiers, and relevant calendar events. The API response may
include event metadata as well as timing. The Service reduces unmanaged events
to start/end times and the information needed to determine whether they block
a slot. It does not persist their titles, descriptions, locations, attendee
lists, or calendar names. The reduced availability information is used in
memory for the scheduling run, rather than stored as a calendar history.

Managed-event information. We process identifiers, scheduling times, organizer
and participant addresses, response status, and app-generated metadata for
events created by the Service. The app stores the identifier of your app-created
calendar on the server. Managed-event details are stored in Google Calendar.

Information you submit. Event forms collect titles, optional descriptions and
locations, attendee addresses, durations, recurrence and date ranges, eligible
days/time windows, priorities, and scheduling preferences. We also receive
information about you if another participant includes your address in a shared
request or a maintainer updates the shared configuration.

Session and technical information. The Service creates sign-in state, browser
session identifiers, expiration times, and request-protection secrets. Its
operational output may include account emails, request identifiers, scheduling
times, and operation or error information. Infrastructure providers may process
IP addresses, request and device metadata, and security logs when you visit the
website. If you email us, we receive the information you include in that message.

2. How information is used

We use this information to:
- Identify the calendar account that authorized access and keep you signed in.
- Display approved requests that include your email and verify whether you may
  propose an edit or removal.
- Validate proposed settings, create GitHub pull requests, record the requester,
  and support maintainer review.
- Calculate compatible availability across participants and reconcile managed
  events with approved configuration.
- Send invitations and updates through Google Calendar, and process declines.
- Diagnose scheduling and connection problems, protect the Service, answer
  support or privacy requests, and comply with legal obligations.

Calendar authorization is separate from scheduling configuration. Connecting
alone does not create a request, publish your identity to GitHub, or authorize
access to other people's accounts. Approved requests determine whose unrelated
calendar events the scheduler needs to read. For a connected account no longer
named in a request, the scheduler may still list calendars and read its
app-created calendar to remove obsolete managed blocks.

3. Google Calendar permissions and limitations

The app requests these OAuth scopes:

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.readonly
This permits reading calendars and events available to the connected account,
including subscribed calendars. Calendar exclusions in configuration can limit
which calendars are used to determine availability.

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.app.created
This permits creating an app-owned calendar and managing events on calendars
the app created. It does not give the app write access to other calendars.

The app uses these permissions to provide the account and scheduling functions
described above. It does not edit unrelated calendar events, upload unrelated
event details or busy-time snapshots to GitHub, or maintain a historical
database of your unrelated calendar contents.

Our use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to
the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements:
https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy

Google-derived information is not sold, used for targeted advertising, or used
to train generalized artificial-intelligence or machine-learning models. Human
access to Google user data is restricted to purposes permitted by that policy,
such as access you authorize, necessary security investigation, and applicable
legal obligations. Revoking Google access prevents future authorized API
access, but does not automatically delete existing records or copies.

4. Public GitHub proposals and participant disclosure

The default configuration repository is public:
https://github.com/dtrager02/calendar-blocker-config

When you submit a create, edit, or removal proposal, the Service sends the
proposed config to GitHub on a separate branch and opens a pull request. The
proposed file contains event settings and participant addresses. The pull
request and commit message also include the verified email obtained during
your calendar connection and your provider identifier so maintainers can see
who requested the change. The commit author may be the operator's GitHub
identity rather than your own.

Publication occurs when the proposal is submitted, before maintainer approval.
It can remain public even if the proposal is rejected. GitHub users, search
engines, and anyone who downloads or copies the repository may access and keep
these records. Although the website filters its event cards by your email,
that filter does not restrict access to the public repository or its history.

By submitting a proposal, you direct this publication of its settings and your
requester identity. Do not submit secrets, confidential information, sensitive
personal information, or contact details you lack permission to publish. Event
names, locations, and time preferences can reveal personal routines. Your
calendar refresh tokens, browser-session secrets, and unrelated calendar-event
contents are not included in these proposals.

The Service also sends managed-event details, timing, and participant addresses
to Google Calendar. Shared events send invitations and updates to participants;
those recipients and their calendar providers may receive the details and
participant identities under their own account settings and policies.

Removing an active request is not the same as erasing public history. Closing
pull requests, deleting branches, or deleting the request from current config
cannot guarantee deletion of commits, repository clones, caches, past calendar
events, or information already received by participants.

6. Essential cookies and temporary form data

The app sets two essential, first-party cookies:

__Host-calendar-oauth: binds an OAuth authorization attempt to the browser that
started it, preventing another browser from completing that attempt. Its
maximum lifetime is 15 minutes, and it is cleared after the callback. Associated
server-side OAuth state is kept in memory and expires after 15 minutes.

__Host-calendar-session: an opaque identifier that keeps your browser signed
in for up to 30 days. The server stores a hash of that identifier together with
your provider, email, expiration time, and a secret used to protect form
submissions from forgery. Signing out invalidates that session and clears its
cookie. Calendar refresh tokens are not stored in browser cookies.

Both cookies use Secure, HttpOnly, and SameSite=Lax settings. They are needed
for sign-in and form security. The application does not add advertising or
analytics cookies or third-party analytics scripts. Infrastructure providers
may independently process technical information for delivery and security.

Form drafts, approved-config snapshots used by those forms, submission
identifiers, and PR receipts are held in server memory. Forms become invalid
after one hour. Expired drafts are removed during later form cleanup, and all
in-memory drafts and receipts are discarded when the server process restarts.

7. Storage, security, and retention

Public website traffic uses HTTPS through Cloudflare. The tunnel's connection
to the app uses the instance's local loopback interface. Google and GitHub API
connections use HTTPS. Calendar credentials, account state, and session files
are kept in the app's private server directory using owner-only file
permissions. These are access controls, not a promise that every stored file
is application-encrypted or that security incidents are impossible.

Different records have different lifetimes:
- Unmanaged calendar-event snapshots are not persisted and are discarded after
  the scheduling run; the next run fetches current information again.
- Refresh tokens, account emails, and app-calendar identifiers stay on the
  server until the operator removes them. Revocation prevents their continued
  use but does not automatically erase these records.
- Browser sessions stop authenticating after expiration. An expired session
  file is deleted when that identifier is checked again or when the operator
  removes it; expiry alone does not trigger a background deletion job.
- Operational logs and the scheduler's local config checkout have no automatic
  app-enforced deletion period. They may remain until operational cleanup or
  a deletion request is handled, subject to legal and security requirements.
- GitHub branches, pull requests, commits, and history remain until removed
  under GitHub's and the repository administrators' controls; independently
  copied public material may remain outside those controls.
- Managed events remain in Google Calendar until the scheduler or an account
  holder deletes them. Removing a request targets future, not already-started,
  managed blocks. It does not erase past events.

We retain information only for the purposes described in this Policy, including
operating the Service and meeting applicable legal, security, and dispute-
resolution obligations. You can request deletion of records under our control
without waiting for an automatic deletion process. No service can guarantee
absolute security or complete recovery or erasure of distributed public data.
